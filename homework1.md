# Home Activity 1: Information Systems Hierarchy and Cost Analysis

## Part 1: Management Levels and Information Flow

### 1. Operational Level (Bottom)

- Mnanagement level: Operational
- Information Sustem: Transaction Processing System (TPS)
- Data Flow and Purpose: This level handles daily business activities such as sales, customer orders and stock records. The TPS collects and stores these transactions accurately. The information helps 
supervisors monitor daily activities and make sure the business runs smoothly.

### 2. Tactical Level (Middle)
- Management level: Tactical
- Information System: Management Information System (MIS) and Decision Support System (DSS)
- Data Flow and Purpose: This level uses information collected from daily operations to prepare reports and analyse business performance. MIS helps managers monitor activities, while DSS helps them compare
options and make better decisions.

### 3. Strategic Level (Top)
- Management Level: Strategic
- Information System: Executive Support System (ESS)
- Data Flow and Purpose: This level uses summarised business information, reports and forecasts to help top executives make long-term decisions. It helps executives identify trends, monitor competitors and 
plan the future direction of the organisation.


## Part 2: Technical Architecture Definitions

### 1. IT Infrastructure Diagram
- An IT Infrastructure Diagram shows the actual physical assets that are deployed in an organisation. These include physical equipment and devices used to support IT operations. It provides details about 
technology resources available in the organisation.

### 2. IT Landscape Diagram
- An IT Landscape Diagram provides a high-level overview of the different IT assets in the organisation. It shows asset types such as hardware, software and connected components at a logical level. It gives 
a simplified view of how these assets support business operations without showing as much physical detail as an infrastructure diagram.

### 3. IT Architecture Diagram
- An IT Architecture Diagram outlines the concepts, principles, norms and standards that an IT landscape must follow. It provides guidelines for how IT systems and components should be structured. It helps 
ensure that the organisation's technology follows the required standards and guidelines.
